## 02. Calculus of Belief

**2. what does $K$ represent?**

$K$ represents what you know at the time you state your probability— your knowledge base.

**3. Create an example where the probability of an event is different when $K$ is different.**

Let's say the probability of a race winning the race is, $x$. The probability, $x$, increases if you are given the information that 40% of the other drives have crashed out and are out of the race.

**4. If $E$ denotes an event, what does $E^c$ denote?**

$E^c$ denotes the complement of that event, or the probability of $E$'s opposite occurring.

**5. Consider a potentially unfair six-sided die.  Let $E$ denote the outcome of a die roll.  If $P(E$ is even $) = .6$, what must $P(E$ is odd $)$ be?  Why?**

Regardless of whether the die is fair or not, there are only two outcomes in this context: odd or even. Therefore, $$P(E \text{ is odd}) = 0.4$$

## Probability Likelihood Chance
1. The series would not be exchangeable if the championship series was played only at team A's stadium because home field advantage would introduce an imbalance in the probability of outcomes.
In an exchangeable series, each possible sequence of outcomes has an equal probability of occurring. This means that the probability of team A winning a game and team B winning a game is the same, regardless of the order of wins and losses. However, if the championship series is played only at team A's stadium, team A is more likely to win games, and this advantage would not be balanced out.
Considering the provided sequences, in a neutral setting, the probability of team A winning a game would be the same as the probability of team B winning a game, and therefore, the sequences would be exchangeable. However, if all the games are played at team A's stadium, team A would have a home field advantage, and the probability of team A winning a game would be higher. **This means that sequences with more wins are more likely and as a result, not an exchangeable situation.**
2. If the championship series is played in alternating stadiums, with Game 1 at Stadium A, Game 2 at Stadium B, Game 3 at Stadium A, and so on, the series would still be exchangeable. When games are played in alternating stadiums, the home field advantage is balanced out, as each team has an equal number of games played in their own stadium. HOWEVER, with only five games, team A will have an advantage given that there are more A home advantage events than B.
3. If the series is played either entirely in Stadium A or entirely in Stadium B, but the specific stadium is unknown, the series would be exchangeable. Exchangeability means that the order of outcomes does not impact the probability assessment of the series, and in this case, it would still hold true. When the specific stadium is unknown and there is an equal chance of the series being played in Stadium A or Stadium B, the probability of each team winning a game is the same for both stadiums. Therefore, the sequences of outcomes are exchangeable because the order of wins and losses does not impact the probability assessment. In this situation, the exchangeability is preserved due to the equal likelihood of either stadium hosting the entire series.
